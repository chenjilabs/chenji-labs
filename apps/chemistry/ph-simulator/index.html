pH simulator

Aim

Aim: find out how the pH of an acid or base depends on its concentration, and why a strong and a weak acid at the same concentration have different pH values.

0 acidic7basic 14

pH 7.00

Assumes 25 °C, one substance dissolved in pure water.

Conditions

  • Temperature 25 °C, so the ionic product of water is Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴.
  • One solute dissolved in pure water.
  • Concentrations are used in place of activities.

What to try

  1. Pick 0.1 mol/L hydrochloric acid, then switch to 0.1 mol/L ethanoic acid (acetic acid). Why is the pH different?

    Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid: it dissociates completely, so [H⁺] is 0.1 mol/L and the pH is 1.00. Ethanoic acid is weak and dissociates only partly, so [H⁺] is much lower and the pH is about 2.88.

  2. Dilute hydrochloric acid by a factor of 10. How much does the pH change?

    The pH rises by 1. [H⁺] falls by a factor of 10, and pH = −log₁₀[H⁺], so each tenfold dilution adds 1 to the pH.

  3. Do the same with ethanoic acid. Is the change the same?

    No. The pH rises by only about 0.5. Dilution lets a larger fraction of a weak acid dissociate, which partly makes up for the lower concentration.

Check your understanding

A solution of hydrochloric acid has pH 2. You dilute it ten times with pure water. What is the new pH?

  • Diluting lowers [H⁺], so the pH goes up, not down.

  • Diluting changes [H⁺], so the pH must change too.

  • [H⁺] falls tenfold, from 0.01 to 0.001 mol/L, so the pH rises by exactly 1.

  • The pH scale is logarithmic: a tenfold change in [H⁺] changes the pH by 1, it does not multiply it by 10.

Which has the lower pH: 0.1 mol/L hydrochloric acid or 0.1 mol/L ethanoic acid?

  • A strong acid releases all of its H⁺, so at the same concentration it has the higher [H⁺] and the lower pH.

  • Only one hydrogen in ethanoic acid is acidic, and it dissociates only partly. The other hydrogens stay bonded to carbon.

  • Concentration and strength are different things. Strength is how completely the acid dissociates.

Science behind this

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]

The simulator finds [H⁺] from a charge balance that includes the ions from water. For a strong acid at concentration c, [H⁺] = c + Kw/[H⁺]. For a weak acid, [H⁺] = Kac/(Ka + [H⁺]) + Kw/[H⁺]. Bases are treated the same way with [OH⁻], and then pH = 14 + log₁₀[OH⁻].

Assumptions

  • 25 °C, so Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴.
  • One solute in pure water.
  • Concentrations are used in place of activities.

Limits

Concentrated solutions deviate from this model, because the ions interact and their activities differ from their concentrations.
